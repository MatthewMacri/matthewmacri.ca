Garmin Connect records a hockey game as one long blob of heart rate. It never splits the shifts from the
time on the bench, which is the part that actually tells you how hard you played. This is a Connect IQ
app that fixes that from the wrist alone.

## What it does

You press start at the opening faceoff and never touch the watch again. From there it works out when
you're on the ice and when you're on the bench by itself, and keeps one continuous game clock running
until you stop it.

- **Automatic shift detection** — no button per line change; the app decides when you're on and off.
- **One continuous game clock** — no periods to set up, nothing that ends the activity on a timer. A real
  game runs longer than the sum of its periods.
- **A shift clock you feel, not read** — the ring around the screen runs green to amber to red as a shift
  runs long, with a full-strength buzz felt through a glove at your target length.
- **On-ice heart rate, kept separate from bench heart rate** — the number the app exists to produce.
- **A post-game summary** written into a real Garmin activity that syncs to Connect like any other sport.

## How it's built

It's written in Monkey C for Connect IQ. I built and tested it on a Forerunner 570, but the layout is
resolution-independent, so a single build covers the 126 watches listed in the manifest — most of the
current Garmin range. It's open source under MIT and published on the Connect IQ store.

## Where it fits

This is the watch end of a small hockey-tracking system. A companion phone app draws the shift data that
Garmin Connect leaves flat, and a separate hardware project adds per-leg skating metrics from sensors in
the shin guards — both described elsewhere in this list.
